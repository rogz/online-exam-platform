Quick start: your first exam in 7 steps

  1. Create an account as a teacher (section 1). New teachers get 5 free credits.
  2. Create a class and send its join link to your students (section 3).
  3. Add questions to your question bank, one by one or from an Excel/CSV file (section 4).
  4. Create an exam: choose the class, the dates, the time limit and the questions (section 5).
  5. Preview it and check the answer key (section 6).
  6. Publish it and post the exam link, e.g. in Google Classroom (section 7).
  7. See the results as students submit (section 8).

1. Create your account and sign in

  1. Open the site and click Create account.
  2. Choose I'm a teacher.
  3. Fill in your full name, email and a password (at least 8 characters). The school name is optional.
  4. If the administrator gave you an access code, type it in. You can also add it later.
  5. Click Create account.

3. Classes

An exam is always given to a class. Students must join the class to see its exams.

Create a class

  1. Click Classes in the menu, then + New class.
  2. Type the class name (e.g. EEE 152) and, if you like, a section.
  3. Click Create class.

Let students join

Tip: post the join link once at the start of the term. Students only need to join once per class.

Fill in the blank with a calculated answer

For a calculation, students rarely get exactly the same digits as you. Give the answer as a range, so rounding along the way still counts.

Typing ±, Ω, °, μ… Click the symbol buttons under the answer box (Insert a symbol): the symbol goes where your cursor is, in the question, a choice or the answer. You can also type +- instead of ±; it works the same. In Excel or a CSV file, +- is the easiest.
You writeCounts as correct

122.5 ± 0.4 V (or +-)the same range, written as ±
122.5 ± 1% Vwithin 1% of 122.5. A good choice for most calculations.
0.7788exactly that number (.7788 and 0.77880 also count)

Students may type 122.48, 122.48 V or 122.48V; the unit is optional. You can mix numbers and words: 122.5 ± 1% | one hundred twenty-two.

Exam Wizard also spots answers that are almost right: a close spelling ("Feranti" for "Ferranti"), a number just outside your range, or a different unit ("kV" instead of "V"). They are marked to check in Results, and you decide (see section 8).

Points for this exam (optional)

If your questions are worth different points (e.g. 2 for calculations, 1 for the rest), a random set can give students different totals: one is out of 34, another out of 36. The Points setting fixes this for one exam, without changing your question bank.

Set this before the exam opens. Results already submitted keep the points they had. Another way to give everyone the same total: pick per question type or per topic (e.g. 26 multiple choice + 4 fill in the blank), so every student gets the same mix.

Random snapshots (optional)

As an extra check, the camera can take small pictures at random times during the exam. Tick Take random snapshots under the camera setting and choose how many per hour (6 by default). The hour is split into equal parts (6 per hour = every 10 minutes) and one picture is taken at a random moment in each part, so students cannot predict them.

  • Students are told automatically on the rules screen before they start (you may also mention it in your instructions).
  • The pictures are small and gray (about 4 KB each), enough to see who is taking the exam and what is on the desk. No video is recorded.
  • You see them in Results → Answers, marked Random check.
  • They are deleted permanently when you archive the exam or the class, and in any case 30 days after the exam closes. Look at them (or save one) before that.

6. Preview and check the answer key

Before publishing, try the exam yourself. Nothing is saved, and the security checks are off.

Answer the questions, then click Check answers at the bottom.

The preview also flags questions with no correct answer marked and pictures that do not load (e.g. a Google Drive picture that is not shared publicly). For fill in the blank, try a slightly different answer too (another rounding, a typo): the preview shows what would be accepted and what would be flagged for you to check.

7. Publish and share the link

  1. In the exam editor, click Save & publish. Publishing an exam the first time uses 1 credit, unless you have days of full access. Editing or re-publishing it later is free.
  2. Click Copy link (in the editor or in the Exams list).
  3. Paste the link where your students will see it. In Google Classroom: Classwork → Create → Assignment → Add → Link.

Students who are signed in and in the class land on the exam. Others are asked to sign in first. The exam also appears by itself on every student's My Exams page.

Before a big exam: ask students to sign in the day before, check that the dates and the time limit are right in the Exams list, and try the Preview once.

9. Archive old exams and classes

  • Archiving an exam tidies your list. Results are kept and students still see their own scores. Open Archived below the list and click Restore to bring one back.
  • Archiving a class (Classes page) hides the class and its exams from students, and its code stops working. Use it at the end of a semester. Restore brings it back.
  • Camera snapshots are deleted when you archive an exam or a class, to free space. You are told how many before you confirm; restoring does not bring them back. Snapshots are also deleted 30 days after an exam closes.
  • Duplicate (in the exam editor) copies an exam, e.g. to give the same quiz to another class.

11. What your students see

Students see every exam from all their classes on one page, My Exams: open now, upcoming, completed and expired.

An offline exam is marked 📥 Offline exam in their list, with a box above the start button: Download it now, while you have internet and a Download for offline button. After downloading it says ✓ Downloaded on this device.

After an exam, if you show scores, the exam shows their results report: the score in big numbers, their rank and the class statistics (marked "current" while classmates are still taking the exam or being graded), and Print my result.

Before starting, they read the rules for your settings and pass a system check (device, browser, monitors, camera).

  • Exams are taken on a laptop or desktop (or a tablet, if you allow it). Phones are for checking the schedule and scores.
  • If the internet drops or the page reloads, the student reopens the same link within the reconnect window and continues. The timer keeps running.
  • When time runs out, answers are submitted automatically.
  • After submitting, students see their score only if you allowed it, and never the correct answers.

12. Weak or no internet

In the exam editor, choose how the exam handles the internet in the room:

Strict is the old rule: the exam is voided if a student is offline longer than the reconnect window.

Unstable internet (online exams)

  • When the internet drops, students see "📶 No internet… keep answering". Their answers are kept on the device ("Saved on this device") and also on the site whenever there is a connection ("✓ Saved").
  • If time runs out while offline, the student sees "Your exam is finished" and the answers are sent by themselves when the internet is back. They count as on time if they were finished in time.
  • In Results you see notes like 📶 Offline 5 min and ⏳ Sent after the internet came back. Nothing is voided for being offline.
  • Closing or reloading the page still follows the reconnect rules (Advanced settings), so students should keep the page open.

Offline exam: before the exam day

  1. In the exam editor choose No internet, or very weak, click Make a strong password, then Save & publish.
  2. Tell students to open the exam link with internet, on the device they will use in the room, and click Download for offline. They see ✓ Ready offline. Ask them to bookmark the page right away.
  1. Check who has downloaded: Results → Offline copies. Remind the others.
If you change the questions or the password after students downloaded the exam, they must download it again (the editor tells you how many already have). Try a short practice quiz in offline mode before a major exam, so students know the steps.

Offline exam: in the room

  1. Students open the exam on the device and in the browser they downloaded it with: their bookmark, the exam link, or quiz.karmsys.com/offline (also shown when they open My Exams without internet). No internet is needed. A device that downloaded the exam always uses its downloaded copy, even when there is internet.
  2. When everyone is ready, say the password. They type it (capital letters and dashes don't matter), tick the rules box and click Start exam.

Finishing: receipt code and QR code

The answers reach you in one of three ways:

  • By themselves, as soon as the student's device is online (the page can be closed; opening the exam link again with internet sends them).
  • QR code: you or a helper scan it with a phone (below). This works even if the student never gets internet.
  • Answer file: the student clicks Save a copy of my answers and gives you the file. Upload it in Results → Offline copies → Upload an answer file.

Collect answers with your phone

  1. On your phone, open Results → Offline copies → Scan answers (QR). Or make a helper link for an assistant: it opens only the scanner for this exam, shows no scores, and stops working at the time you choose.
  2. Tap Start scanning and point the camera at the student's QR code. A beep and ✓ Received mean it is done. Then the next student.
  3. No internet on the phone? Keep scanning. The answers are kept on the phone and sent by themselves later. Keep the page open until "waiting" is 0.

After the exam: check

Open Results. Offline copies shows who has not sent answers yet. Each offline attempt shows an Offline tag and its receipt code: compare it with your sheet. Some attempts show ⚠ Check:

NoteWhat it means
The receipt code does not matchThe answers may have been changed after the student finished. Compare with the code on your sheet.
The device clock was changed / looked wrongThe student's clock was moved during the exam, or its date was wrong.
Finished … after the time was upThe answers were finished after the time limit.
Arrived … after the exam closedThe answers were sent later than your limit (see the first picture of this section).

For the site owner: in Admin → Site settings → Safety, tick Pause site updates during exam week, so nothing about the site changes during your exams. Updates also wait by themselves while students are taking an exam. A backup of everything is made every night; download one now and then.
